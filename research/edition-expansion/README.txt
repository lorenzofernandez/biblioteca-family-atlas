Biblioteca edition and rarity expansion — checked 2026-10-17

All 49,780 family catalogue records were screened against an additional 2,650-edition historical bibliography. The existing 2,582 automated candidate records were prioritized for direct source retrieval: 2,893 of 2,904 unique Internet Archive source records returned validated metadata, covering 2,580 family records. An unavailable endpoint is not a rejected book identity.

263 catalogue records now have individual edition reviews. There are 229 verified bibliographic connections in total, up from 12; these include explicitly scoped constituent volumes, not assertions that the complete family sets have been physically inspected. Decisions, comparisons, discrepancies, sources and dates are embedded in the book-record shards. The retained review inputs are in this directory.

248 records carry attributed qualitative scarcity assessments. 196 are applicable tentative signals for the family edition at the current scope. Other-edition and partial-set cautions are excluded from classification. Together with the earlier census, the default 100-copy threshold displays 197 potentially rare entries. These are low-confidence signals, not 197 census-confirmed rare books.

305 records have rarity or survival evidence of some kind; 62 have documented copy floors. Located copies are never extrapolated into a global population. Sage's 1811 first edition gains conditional logical bounds of 1–500 based on an author's reported original print run. That is neither a likely surviving range nor 500 known survivors. The full catalogue still lacks enough evidence for a comprehensive numerical rare/non-rare split.

Schuh's bibliography concentrates on historical mineralogical literature and frequently labels editions scarce; it is not a representative sample of the library. Source judgments can concern complete sets, market availability or bibliographical experience. Their confidence is kept separate from edition matching and from known-copy evidence.
